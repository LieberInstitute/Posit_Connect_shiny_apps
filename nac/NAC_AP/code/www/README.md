# Nucleus Acumbens anterior-posterior - Visium HD

Reward-related behaviors are controlled by transcriptionally distinct populations of medium spiny neurons (MSNs) in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) whose topography varies across the medial-lateral and dorsal-ventral axes. However, how the topography and molecular heterogeneity of these cells changes across the NAc’s anterior-posterior axis within the human brain is not known. Here, we generated a three-dimensional molecular neuroanatomical reconstruction of the human NAc using spatial transcriptomics data from serial tissue sections. This 3D atlas localized the spatial topography of cell types across the AP axis, revealing dynamic changes in cell type proportion and local microenvironments. We identified two distinct populations of MSNs expressing the D1 dopamine receptor that organize into -island-like structures. D1-islands show changes in their relative proportions and their proximity to other inhibitory neuron subtypes across the AP axis. Finally,comparative analyses to data from lower-order mammals revealed remarkable conservation of D1-islands across species. Together, these results identify D1-islands as a highly conserved and translationally relevant neuronal subtype.
